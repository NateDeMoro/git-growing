## Contract fields used (src/lib/contract.ts on main)
Read contract.ts before writing anything. Conform to it exactly and never rename. If a field below is missing, ask the coordinator before adding it, and only add.
- SceneFrame: time (ISO, America/Phoenix offset), layers, summary, sun elevation, cloud %, rain rate (in/hr), gusts, surface pressure plus its history or a 1-hour tendency, drift vector, and the sim-only look-ahead and beat fields.
- Layer: height ft, temperature, wind speed, wind direction. Units are whatever contract.ts says (it is expected to hold metric or UI units internally; do not convert twice).
- Summary: inversion F (30 ft minus 5 ft), speed shear, direction shear, Delta T, RH.
- Drift vector: bearing (downwind of the 30 ft wind) and strength 0 to 1.
- Week file wrapper: frames, beats (label, frame index, short description) and a meta block (source week, seed, calibration k, "simulated storm" note).
- Site: tower lat/lon and timezone from `src/lib/site.ts`. Never hardcode coordinates in the scripts.

## Ordered steps
1. **Read site.** Import the tower point from `site.ts`. If W7 has not pinned it yet, site.ts holds 32.61, -114.64. Everything below runs again after the pin lands.
2. **Calibrate.**
   - Pull hourly 2024-01-01 to 2026-11-01 from historical-forecast-api.open-meteo.com: temperature 2 m and 80 m, wind speed and direction 10 m and 80 m, RH, cloud, precipitation, gusts, surface pressure. Use metric units and the America/Phoenix timezone. Chunk by year if one request is too large.
   - Wind: for each hour fit a power-law exponent from the 10 m and 80 m speeds. Clamp it to a sane range (about 0.05 to 0.6) and use the median when either speed is near zero. Direction: blend linearly in log height between 10 m and 80 m on the shortest arc. Below 10 m, keep the 10 m direction.
   - Temperature: 5 ft is the 2 m value. Other heights use log-height interpolation between 2 m and 80 m, with the departure from the 2 m value scaled by k.
   - Fit k by bisection so the median nightly maximum (30 ft minus 5 ft) is 2.5 C. Nightly means sunset to 1 hour after sunrise, by suncalc. Count only nights with a positive raw 80 m minus 2 m gradient.
   - If k comes out outside about 0.3 to 3, refit with the same method on Battany's 2 to 3 C band and record which path ran.
   - Write k, the median exponent, the share of night hours inverted, the median nightly maximum inversion, the date range, the site point and a `fallback` flag. Round to 3 decimals. No timestamp of the run, so the file stays stable.
3. **Derive.**
   - Export one function that maps an hourly record and the calibration to a SceneFrame, plus small helpers for the power law, log-height interpolation and angular difference. Reuse W2's or Phase 0's unit helpers if they are on main, else keep local ones private.
   - Layers come out sorted by height ascending.
   - Summary comes from the 5 ft and 30 ft layers. Delta T uses Stull wet bulb at 5 ft. If W2's wetbulb.ts isn't merged, inline Stull and leave a note to switch to W2's on rebase.
   - Sun elevation from suncalc at the tower point. Rain rate is hourly precipitation in mm converted to in/hr. The drift strength rises with inversion strength and wind speed; any simple bounded formula is fine (presentation only).
   - Use when: W5 forecast and the live `/api/now` route need a frame from an Open-Meteo hour.
4. **Pick the week.** Choose a recent real November week (7 days ending before 2026-11-01, or the latest historical-forecast days available). Prefer one with clear calm nights. Record the dates in meta. Cache the pull.
5. **Build the sim.**
   - Derive the 168 hourly frames, then interpolate to 5-minute steps: linear for scalars, shortest-arc for directions. That gives 2,016 frames.
   - Add noise from a fixed-seed PRNG (mulberry32 or similar, seed in meta): small wobble on temperature and wind, none on beat-critical values.
   - Overlay the beats as smooth blended overrides on top of the base week. Each beat gets a label, a start frame index and a peak frame index:
     - **A:** day 1 evening. 5 ft at 5 mph and 72 F, 30 ft 3 F warmer. Delta T inside 2 to 10 C, no rain, and humidity set so the station-only run stays GO. The timing hint also runs in single-station mode, so hold cloud above 25% at A (see Risks) and confirm against W2's engine once merged.
     - **B:** next morning, the inversion breaks about 1 hour after sunrise. Winds 4 to 8 mph give a GO window of at least 2 hours.
     - **C:** afternoon, 30 ft ramps past 12 mph while 5 ft stays near 6 mph.
     - **D:** direction difference of 120 degrees between 5 ft and 30 ft, both speeds 4 mph or more.
     - **E:** calm, clear evening (cloud 25% or less), the timing hint fires about 3 hours before sunset, then the inversion grows past 1.8 F.
     - **F:** mid-week, about 12 sim-hours, meta labels it "simulated storm". Cloud climbs to 100%. Pressure falls about 6 hPa in total, with about 2 hours at 2.0 hPa/hr or faster. Wind goes from 6 to 25 mph with gusts near 40, and 30 ft swings about 90 degrees ahead of 5 ft. Rain goes from drizzle to about 0.4 in/hr, temperature drops about 12 F, then clears back toward CAUTION.
   - Look-ahead: every frame carries the next beat (label and frame index) and the frame index 3 sim-hours later (36 frames). **Status 3 hours later is left for runtime.** Status depends on the selected product, W2's engine runs in parallel and is not on main, and frames must hold readings only (plan 5b). W6 computes it on the client with assess. If contract.ts has a status slot, leave it unset; do not write a placeholder.
   - Write a 48-frame fixture to `tests/fixtures/frames48.json`: 4 hours around Beat A plus the start of Beat F, chosen so W4 and W7 get both an inversion and storm values. Include the beats array so their playback bar works.
6. **Determinism.** No `Date.now`, no `Math.random`, and no machine-timezone formatting (format with fixed offsets). Fixed key order. Numbers rounded at write: temperatures and speeds to 1 decimal, directions to whole degrees, pressure to 1 decimal, rain to 2, strength to 2. Run the build twice and compare hashes.
7. **Re-run after the pin.** When W7's site.ts lands on main, rebase, run calibrate and build-sim again, check the beats still hold, and commit the new outputs.

## Risks
- **Contract drift:** the sim needs fields (look-ahead, beats, pressure tendency) that Phase 0 may have shaped differently. Read contract.ts first and ask before adding.
- **Beat A vs the timing hint:** the single-station run must say GO, but the evening hint adds CAUTION from 3 hours before sunset when cloud is 25% or less. Put cloud above 25% at Beat A, or place it where the hint is off. Confirm against W2's engine on rebase.
- **Engine not merged:** beats are tuned by numbers, not verdicts. Leave the integration test to Phase 2 and expect one retune pass then.
- **Historical API limits or gaps:** chunk requests, cache raw data, and fail loudly on nulls rather than writing NaN.
- **k out of range at Yuma:** desert nights may give strong raw gradients. The fallback path exists; record which path ran.
- **Site pin timing:** outputs from 32.61, -114.64 are fine to merge but must be regenerated after the pin.
- **Lockfile conflicts:** add no runtime dependencies. If you add tsx as a dev dependency, follow plan section 9.
- **Byte drift across machines:** floating-point formatting and timezone use are the usual causes. Round explicitly and avoid locale formatting.
